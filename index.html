Welcome toAkoli Speech

Your voice. Your words. Better understood.

Akoli Speech is a personalised speech recognition app designed for people whose speech may be difficult for conventional speech recognition systems to understand.

By learning from your speech, Akoli aims to help technology better recognise your words, making communication more accessible.

How Akoli works

Speech recognition that learns from you

Everyone speaks differently. Yet most speech recognition technology is designed around a limited range of voices.

Akoli takes a different approach.

  1. Record your speechCreate recordings at your own pace.
  2. Build your modelAkoli uses your recordings to develop a personalised speech recognition model.
  3. Use your voiceThe app aims to recognise your speech more accurately and turn your words into text.

Built on research from Google's Project Euphonia, Akoli is helping explore how speech technology can work for more people.

Join the Akoli Community

The Akoli Community

Better speech technology, shaped by the people who use it.

We believe that people whose speech is often misunderstood should be central to shaping the technology we design.

The Akoli Community will bring together people with different speech patterns, families, researchers, developers and disability organisations to learn from one another and help shape more inclusive speech technology.

There are different ways to get involved:

Help shape Akoli

  • Share your experiences of using speech technology.
  • Join user testing and co-design activities.
  • Tell us what works, what doesn't, and what matters most.

Help build the future

  • Collaborate on research and innovation.
  • Explore opportunities to contribute to inclusive speech recognition.
  • Help expand access across languages and communities.

Akoli Speech is developed at the Centre for Digital Language Inclusion (CDLI) at UCL's Global Disability Innovation Hub (GDI Hub), in collaboration with Talking Tips Africa and Senses Hub.

The development of Akoli Speech has been supported by Google.org, the UK International Development funded AT2030 Programme that tests ‘what works’ to improve access to life-changing Assistive Technology (AT) for all, and global law firm Hogan Lovells provided pro bono legal advice.
